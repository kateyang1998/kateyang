Kate's Sweet Shop Website

Design System & UI Design • Independent Project

Project Overview

Kate's Sweet Shop is a fictional cake shop website, and the focus of this project was building a comprehensive design system to power it. The system defines the brand's foundation styles and a full library of reusable UI components, ensuring visual cohesion, accessibility, and a consistent experience across every page. I then applied the system to design the homepage. The brand centers on warmth, elegance, and deliciousness, expressed through a brown-and-cream palette and refined typography.

The Design System

Foundation Styles

The foundation styles establish the brand's visual language — the building blocks every component is made from.

  • Color Palette: Warm browns (#322323, #5D4037, #8D6E63) for navigation, buttons, and hierarchy; creams (#D7CCC8, #F5F5F5, #FFFFFF) for backgrounds and cards; accent pink (#F8BBD0) and gold (#FFD700) for badges; plus text and functional (success/error) colors.
  • Typography: Playfair Display (elegant serif) for headings and Inter (clean sans-serif) for body, with a full type scale from H1 (48px) to caption (12px).
  • Elevations & Shadows: A five-level shadow system that creates depth and hierarchy — from buttons and cards to navigation, modals, and overlays.
  • Icon System: A consistent icon set (Iconify) with defined sizes (16–32px), color guidelines, and aria-labels for accessibility.

Components

Building on the foundations, I designed a library of reusable components, each with clearly documented states for consistent, predictable behavior.

  • Buttons: Primary, secondary, and tertiary — each with normal, hover, active, disabled, and focus states.
  • Form Elements: Checkboxes, radio buttons, text inputs (with label, focus, error, and success states), and select/dropdown menus.
  • Cards: Product cards with image, optional badge, title, description, price, and actions.
  • Navigation: A responsive navbar with logo, links, action icons, and cart badge, plus a mobile hamburger menu.

Accessibility

Accessibility was built into the system from the start. Color combinations were checked against WCAG contrast standards, and icons were made screen-reader friendly.

  • Primary Dark (#322323) on white — 14.8:1 contrast (WCAG AAA).
  • Primary (#5D4037) on white — 9.2:1 contrast (WCAG AAA).
  • Secondary text (#757575) on white — 4.6:1 contrast (WCAG AA).
  • All icons include aria-labels, with text labels added when meaning is ambiguous.

Applying the System

To demonstrate the system in action, I designed the homepage entirely from its foundations and components. Every element — from the navigation and buttons to the product cards and order form — is drawn from the design system, showing how it creates a cohesive, on-brand experience.

Homepage Sections
  • Hero: "For Every Sweet Moment," featuring a signature cake and clear calls to action.
  • Choose Your Flavour: Flavour and dietary filters built from the form components.
  • Our Featured Cakes: A grid of product cards with badges, prices, and actions.
  • Order Your Lovely Cake Now: A detailed order form with delivery options and add-ons.
  • About & Footer: Brand story plus contact, business hours, and quick links.

Final Solution & Design Brief

The final result is a complete design system — and a homepage built from it — that gives Kate's Sweet Shop a warm, elegant, and consistent identity while remaining accessible and scalable for future pages.

Core Focus Areas

Cohesive Brand Identity, Reusable Components, and Accessibility

Cohesive Brand Identity

Goal: A warm, elegant, deliciously on-brand feel.

Solution: A brown-and-cream palette and refined serif/sans-serif typography.

Impact: A consistent look and feel across the whole site.

Reusable Components

Goal: Build pages quickly and consistently.

Solution: A documented library of components with defined states.

Impact: Faster design and predictable, cohesive UI.

Accessibility

Goal: Usable and inclusive by default.

Solution: WCAG-compliant contrast and screen-reader-friendly icons.

Impact: An experience that works for more people.

Design Principles Applied

  • Consistency: A single source of truth for colors, type, and components
  • Visual Hierarchy: Elevation and typography scale that guide attention
  • Accessibility: WCAG-aligned contrast and semantic, labeled icons
  • Scalability: A flexible system that can grow to new pages and features

Reflect

What I Learned

  • How to build a comprehensive design system from foundations to components
  • Documenting component states and usage for consistency and reuse
  • Embedding accessibility into design decisions from the very start

Next Steps

If I were to continue this project, I would focus on:

  • Designing the remaining pages (products, cart, and checkout) from the system
  • Turning the components into coded, reusable front-end components
  • Adding responsive specifications and interaction/motion guidelines
