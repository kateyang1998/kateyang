Recipe Mobile App

UX Design • Independent Project

Project Overview

The Recipe Mobile App helps users cook smarter with the ingredients they already have. It lets users browse recipes based on their available ingredients, track ingredient expiration dates to reduce food waste, and automatically generate a weekly grocery shopping list from saved recipes. I designed the app independently, applying cognitive psychology, Don Norman's design principles, and UX laws to create an intuitive and effective experience.

User Research

I began by identifying the app's primary users and the challenges they face around cooking and meal planning. Two groups stood out: busy working parents who want to cook for their families using what's already in the fridge, and health-conscious students who need recipes that fit their dietary goals. Common themes emerged across both groups.

Key Themes
  • Limited time for meal planning and cooking due to busy schedules.
  • Difficulty coming up with recipes from the ingredients on hand.
  • Food waste caused by forgotten, expiring ingredients.
  • A need for recipes that match dietary restrictions and nutritional goals.

Problem Analysis

Drawing on the personas, I identified the core problems the app needed to solve.

1. Time Constraints

Busy users struggle to plan meals and cook with limited time after work or study.

2. Ingredient Waste

Forgotten, expiring ingredients lead to food waste, added cost, and food safety risks.

3. Recipe Discovery

It's hard to find recipes that fit both the ingredients on hand and users' dietary and nutritional goals.

Problem Statement

Busy home cooks and health-conscious users want to prepare meals easily using the ingredients they already have, but they struggle to find suitable recipes, track expiring ingredients, and plan meals around their dietary goals. The Recipe App needs to make cooking effortless by recommending recipes from available ingredients, reducing food waste, and simplifying meal and grocery planning.

Ideate

Sketches

With the personas and the project's design challenges defined, I sketched initial concepts to explore the app's core screens and flows — from ingredient-based search to meal planning and the shopping list.

Prototype

Low-Fidelity Prototypes

I translated the sketches into low-fidelity wireframes in Figma, combining the personas' needs with the key design challenges to finalize the necessary screens — Home, Search, Kitchen, Meal Planner, Recipe, Shopping List, and User Profile. The focus was on clear structure, navigation, and visual hierarchy.

High-Fidelity Prototypes

I then developed interactive high-fidelity prototypes with a clean, accessible interface, refining the visual design, iconography, and layout. The design emphasizes clarity, easy navigation, and consistent components for an intuitive experience.

Final Solution & Design Brief

The final solution is an ingredient-first recipe app that makes cooking smarter and reduces food waste. It integrates cognitive psychology, Don Norman's design principles, and UX laws to deliver an intuitive, accessible experience across all core screens.

Core Features

Ingredient-Based Recipes, Expiration Tracking, and Smart Meal & Shopping Planning

Ingredient-Based Recipe Search

Problem: It's hard to cook with only what's on hand.

Solution: Search or scan ingredients to generate recipes, with filters for meal type, cuisine, difficulty, and prep time.

Impact: Makes the most of existing ingredients and saves time.

Expiration Tracking

Problem: Forgotten ingredients expire and go to waste.

Solution: A Kitchen screen tracks ingredients and sends notifications before they expire.

Impact: Reduces food waste, cost, and food safety risks.

Meal Planning & Shopping List

Problem: Planning healthy meals for the week is tedious.

Solution: Generate a weekly meal plan and an automatic shopping list from saved recipes, with nutritional info and dietary profiles.

Impact: Simplifies planning and supports users' dietary goals.

Design Principles Applied

  • Visibility (Norman): Clear icons, straightforward navigation, and uncluttered screens
  • Feedback (Norman): Timely alerts, such as ingredient expiration notifications
  • Affordance & Mapping (Norman): Intuitive icons and controls that respond predictably to user actions
  • Constraints & Consistency (Norman): Preventing user errors and keeping buttons, icons, and layouts uniform
  • Fitts's Law: Large, full-width search bars and buttons for easy, accurate tapping
  • Gestalt Principles: Similarity, Proximity, and Common Region to group related content
  • Aesthetic-Usability Effect: A visually pleasing design that feels easier to use
  • Accessibility: Color choices for visual impairments and audio cues for hearing impairments
