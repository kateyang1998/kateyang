Renovation Consultant Website

UX Design • Independent Project

Project Overview

This project designed an official website for a non-profit, non-government renovation consultant organization. The organization helps commercial building owners with renovation and preservation, while also engaging the community through events, donations, volunteering, and educational materials on preservation and local history. The website was designed with a user-centered philosophy, respecting different social roles and cultural backgrounds, and following AODA accessibility principles.

Duration

1 week

Team

Independent Project

My Role

UX Researcher, Logo & Brand Designer, UI/UX Designer, Prototype Designer (end-to-end)

Tools

Adobe Illustrator, Figma

Empathize

Client Needs & Goals

The organization needed a website that lets target users learn about its business and events, download or subscribe to tutorials on preservation and local history, donate funds, time, materials, or equipment, and schedule volunteer time. All privacy-related features are password-protected behind login.

Core Website Goals
  • Help users find and select suitable services and activities.
  • Enable convenient online donations of funds, time, materials, or equipment.
  • Provide downloadable and subscribable preservation and local-history tutorials.
  • Let volunteers schedule their time online without conflicts.

Research

I researched Waterloo's local context across three factors — history, local business, and education. Since the organization's primary users are owners and managers of commercial buildings, I chose to focus the design on the local business factor, while still respecting users' diverse social roles and cultural backgrounds.

Local Context
  • History: Waterloo's Indigenous heritage and settler history shape its cultural identity.
  • Local Business: A thriving business hub — Sun Life, BlackBerry, D2L, Google, TD, Deloitte — matching the primary target users. (Primary design focus.)
  • Education: A highly educated city home to UW, Wilfrid Laurier, and Conestoga College.

Define

Target Market

The target market is broad, but the primary users are owners and managers of commercial buildings — the population the organization provides renovation services to. Secondary users include volunteers, donors, and citizens interested in preservation and local history. Because of this diversity, the design prioritizes the modules most relevant to the primary users while still giving everyone a good experience.

Technical Standards

I set the visual standards to keep the brand cohesive: a serif typeface (Times New Roman) for a trustworthy, established feel, and a white, green (#00680A), and black palette carried across the logo, navigation, and pages. The navigation covers Home, Login/Register, Services, Events, Donations, Volunteers, Online Tutorials, Contact, and Careers.

Ideate

Logo Design

I designed the organization's logo — a house formed from two halves, one black and one green, with an "RC" (Renovation Consultant) monogram, a window, a bare tree, and a full green shrub. The split house and the contrast between the bare tree and the lush shrub represent renovation and renewal, while the green reinforces the organization's sustainability and preservation values.

Prototype

Low-Fidelity Prototypes

I created low-fidelity wireframes for the Home and Services pages to establish the layout, navigation, and content hierarchy — prioritizing the modules most relevant to the primary users while keeping the rest accessible without stealing attention.

High-Fidelity Prototypes

I then developed high-fidelity prototypes of the Home and Services pages, applying the white-green-black palette and serif typography with a consistent header, navigation, and footer. The design keeps the experience clean, trustworthy, and inclusive for every user.

Final Solution & Design Brief

The final solution is a clean, trustworthy website that helps commercial building owners find renovation services while inviting the wider community to donate, volunteer, and learn about preservation and local history. A consistent brand — logo, green palette, and serif type — ties the pages together.

Core Focus Areas

Services Discovery, Community Engagement, and Inclusive, Accessible Design

Services Discovery

Problem: Building owners need to find suitable renovation services quickly.

Solution: Prioritized service modules and clear navigation aimed at the primary users.

Impact: Faster, more confident decisions for clients like William.

Community Engagement

Problem: Donors and volunteers need easy ways to get involved.

Solution: Online donations, volunteer scheduling, events, and subscribable tutorials.

Impact: More participation from users like Alex.

Inclusive & Secure

Problem: Diverse users and private data need care.

Solution: AODA-aligned, user-centered design with login-protected privacy features.

Impact: A welcoming, trustworthy experience for everyone.

Design Principles Applied

  • Visual Identity: A custom logo and green-based brand system reflecting renewal and sustainability
  • Consistency: Uniform header, navigation, footer, typography, and color across pages
  • Visual Hierarchy: Prioritizing the modules most relevant to the primary users
  • Accessibility (AODA): An inclusive, user-centered design that respects diverse social and cultural backgrounds

Reflect

What I Learned

  • Designing a brand and website end-to-end, from research and logo to high-fidelity prototypes
  • Prioritizing content for a primary user group without neglecting a broad, diverse audience
  • Applying AODA accessibility and user-centered principles to a real client brief

Next Steps

If I were to continue this project, I would focus on:

  • Designing the remaining pages (Donations, Volunteers, Events, Tutorials)
  • Building responsive layouts for tablet and mobile
  • Testing the donation and volunteer scheduling flows with real users
