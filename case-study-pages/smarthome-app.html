Smart Home Mobile App

UX Design • Team Project (4 members)

Project Overview

This project focuses on designing a smart home mobile application that enhances home safety, energy efficiency, and irrigation management through intuitive UX design. The app aims to help environmentally conscious users, busy individuals, and seniors manage their homes efficiently with automated control and real-time monitoring features.

Duration

2 weeks

Team

Kate Yang, Jiwoo Lee, Matt Robinson, Zack Clarke

My Role

UX Researcher (Market Research), Prototype Designer (Sketches and High-Fidelity Prototypes)

Tools

Typeform, Figma

We began by researching the global smart home market and user attitudes toward home automation. Our market analysis highlighted rapid growth, with smart home adoption expected to reach $338.28 billion by 2030. Through an online survey, we explored users’ familiarity with smart technologies, daily challenges, and safety concerns.

User Interviews

Matt conducted semi-structured interviews with two smart home users to better understand their experiences managing irrigation, energy use, and safety at home.

Interview Insights

Key Findings
  • Users want automation that saves time and energy.
  • Safety and peace of mind are top priorities.
  • Setup complexity and data privacy are major concerns.
User Pain Points
  • Manual irrigation adjustments are time-consuming.
  • Difficult setup and integration across devices.
  • Forgetting to turn off appliances poses safety risks.

Empathy Map

Our empathy mapping revealed that users seek convenience, automation, and safety but often feel overwhelmed by the complexity of existing systems. They want clear feedback, control, and reassurance when managing their homes.

Problem Analysis

Based on research and interviews, we identified key stages in the user journey where frustration occurs — from awareness and setup to daily use. Users struggle with complex interfaces, confusing installation, and lack of educational resources.

1. Lack of Awareness

Users do not fully understand the benefits of smart home systems or how they can improve safety and efficiency.

2. Complex Setup

Technical installation and unclear instructions discourage adoption, especially for non-tech-savvy users.

3. Poor Usability

Existing apps have unintuitive interfaces, making it difficult to control devices or monitor energy and water usage efficiently.

Problem Statement

Homeowners and renters, especially those who are busy, eco-conscious, or care for seniors, lack accessible and user-friendly smart home systems to manage their environments safely and efficiently. Current solutions are often overly complex, leading to wasted energy, water, and safety risks.

Ideate

Sketches

Our team collaboratively explored multiple design directions. Each member contributed unique ideas for smart home control, automation flows, and safety alert systems, which were merged into one cohesive concept.

Prototype

Low-Fidelity Prototypes

Jiwoo created digital wireframes in Figma based on selected sketches to refine the layout and navigation flow. The focus was on simplicity, intuitive grouping, and clear visual hierarchy.

High-Fidelity Prototypes

I transformed the wireframes into high-fidelity prototypes with interactive elements for testing. The design emphasized accessibility, easy navigation, and consistent iconography for faster learning.

Final Solution & Design Brief

The final solution integrates smart irrigation, appliance control, and safety monitoring in one cohesive system. The design is human-centered, scalable, and user-friendly for different levels of tech literacy.

Core Solution Areas

Automation, Safety, and Sustainability

Smart Irrigation Scheduling

Problem: Users struggle to manually manage watering schedules.

Solution: Automated watering based on soil moisture and weather data.

Impact: Saves up to 35% water usage and reduces maintenance effort.

Appliance Safety Control

Problem: Forgotten appliances create fire and energy risks.

Solution: Automatic shut-off, real-time alerts, and remote access.

Impact: Prevents accidents and enhances peace of mind.

Simple, Accessible Interface

Problem: Smart home systems often feel complex and inconsistent.

Solution: Unified, easy-to-navigate layout with consistent icons and feedback.

Impact: Reduces user frustration and increases adoption rate.

Design Principles Applied

  • Fitts’s Law: Enlarged main buttons like “Login” and “Create Scene” for quick access
  • Law of Proximity: Grouped related controls together for clear hierarchy
  • Law of Similarity: Consistent shapes and colors to indicate related actions
  • Design Thinking: Followed an iterative process of empathizing, defining, ideating, prototyping, and testing

Reflect

What I Learned

  • How to apply design thinking principles from research to prototype testing
  • The importance of accessibility and clarity in UX for smart technologies
  • Collaborating effectively in a multidisciplinary design team

Next Steps

If I were to continue this project, I would focus on:

  • Conducting usability tests with a wider demographic (especially seniors)
  • Improving onboarding to simplify setup for first-time users
  • Integrating real-time IoT device simulation for more accurate testing
